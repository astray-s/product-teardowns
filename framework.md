# Teardown Framework

Every teardown in this repo follows the same structure. The goal is to build a repeatable muscle for product thinking — not just listing what a product does, but understanding *why* it works or doesn't.

---

## The Framework

### 1. Problem
What is the actual user problem this product solves? Not the feature list — the underlying pain or desire. Ask: what did people do before this existed? What was hard, slow, or impossible?

### 2. What Works
2–3 things the product does well. For each one:
- Describe the specific design or product decision
- Explain *why* it works (the user psychology or system thinking behind it)
- Note what it reveals about the team's product instincts

### 3. What I'd Change
2–3 specific critiques. For each one:
- Name the problem clearly
- Propose a concrete fix
- Explain why the current approach falls short and why your fix is better
- Be specific enough that an engineer could spec it

### 4. Success Metrics
For your most important proposed change:
- What's the primary metric you'd move?
- What's the secondary metric?
- What's the guardrail metric (what you'd make sure you don't break)?

### 5. PM Insight
One sentence. What does this teardown reveal about good product thinking in general?

---

## Why this structure?

The framework mirrors how PMs actually think about products in practice:
- **Problem** = user research and market understanding
- **What works** = competitive analysis and design literacy  
- **What I'd change** = roadmap prioritization and spec thinking
- **Metrics** = data-driven decision making
- **Insight** = synthesis and pattern recognition

The goal isn't to criticize products — it's to build the habit of seeing *why* things are built the way they are, and what tradeoffs were made.
